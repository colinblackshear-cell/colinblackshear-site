Five fixes that stop the ringing

Most fake loan and fake Amazon calls come from scammers who ignore the Do Not Call list, so blocking on the phone itself is what actually makes it quiet. They're in order of impact. Mark each one done as you finish it.

Habits that keep you off the "live number" lists

  • Don't answer, press 1, or say "yes" to unknown callers. Any response marks your number as live, and it gets sold again.
  • Amazon never calls you about an order or a charge. Hang up and check the Amazon app instead. The same goes for your bank, the IRS and Medicare.
  • If a home-repair text is from a real local company, reply STOP; they're legally required to honor it. If it looks like a scam (odd link, no company name), don't reply. Forward it to 7726 and delete it.
  • Give stores, forms and contractors a second number instead of your real one (see "Going forward" below).

Get removed from data brokers

These sites publish your phone number, address and relatives, then sell them to marketers and lead sellers. For most, you search your name, open your own listing, and request removal. Start with the ones tagged Phone. Listings tend to come back, so a re-check reminder appears here three months after you finish each one.

Use one email address just for opt-outs. If a form insists on a phone number, give your second number. Never pay a site or service to remove you; every opt-out here is free.

Going forward

Keep your real number private

A free second number

Google Voice gives you a free number that rings your phone. Use it for stores, rewards cards, contractors and online forms. If it gets spammy, you can change it without touching your real one.

Email aliases

iCloud's Hide My Email, Gmail's you+store@gmail.com trick, or Firefox Relay show you exactly who sold your address.
